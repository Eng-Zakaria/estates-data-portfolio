# Workshop: data literacy for estates

**Length:** 45 minutes
**Group size:** 6 to 20 people, mixed roles
**Room:** a room with a screen and the dashboards open; everyone should be able to see the screen
**Materials:** the space utilisation, energy and carbon, compliance and data quality reports; the [glossary](../generated/glossary.md); the [metric registry](../generated/metric-registry.md); copies of the [quiz](quiz.md) and [feedback form](feedback-form.md)

**Data:** everything is synthetic. Say so at the start.

## Learning outcomes

By the end, participants can:

1. state what a metric measures, in one sentence, without reading the definition;
2. pick the right grain and filter for a decision, and say what a number cannot tell them;
3. spot three common misreadings in an estates dashboard;
4. read a data quality score and decide whether to trust a number;
5. name who owns a definition and how to ask for a change;
6. leave with a habit: quote the definition and the date with every number.

## 0:00 to 0:05 - Why this matters

Say: every estates decision is now argued with numbers, and most arguments are not about the estate, they are about what the number means.

Three things to cover:

- **The data is made up.** Same method, no real estate. Nobody should leave thinking these are our buildings.
- **The rule of the day:** quote the definition and the date with the number.
- **What is at stake:** a utilisation number used to close a space, or an overdue count used to say we are compliant, when neither number means that.

## 0:05 to 0:12 - Exercise 1: three questions

Read [exercise 1](exercises.md#exercise-1-three-questions-a-number-must-answer) aloud. Groups of two or three, seven minutes.

Debrief in three minutes. The point is not the right answer; it is that "I need the definition" is a valid answer, and finding the definition takes under a minute in the [registry](../generated/metric-registry.md).

## 0:12 to 0:24 - Exercise 2: reading a dashboard

Read [exercise 2](exercises.md#exercise-2-reading-a-dashboard-without-being-misled). Twelve minutes, in the room with the reports.

Debrief for five minutes using the answers in [exercise 2](exercises.md#exercise-2-reading-a-dashboard-without-being-misled). Push for the misreading, not just the value.

## 0:24 to 0:33 - Exercise 3: how good is the data

Read [exercise 3](exercises.md#exercise-3-how-good-is-the-data). Nine minutes.

Debrief for four minutes. Land on the sentence: a score measures the checks we wrote, so a high score is a reason to ask what was checked.

## 0:33 to 0:40 - Definitions, owners and change

Cover these points:

- Every published metric has an owner. Only that owner can change the definition.
- A change to a published number is logged, with the reason and the impact.
- Anyone can ask for a change, through the [data request process](../process/data-request.md). Requests that need a new definition take longer on purpose.
- Data classification: internal, public, confidential, and why estates analytics does not use personal data.

Ask the room: "name a number you have quoted in the last month". Then: "could you say its definition and its date?" That is the moment the workshop usually lands.

## 0:40 to 0:45 - Quiz and feedback

Hand out the [quiz](quiz.md), five minutes, silent, in pairs. Answers in the [answer key](answer-key.md). Collect the [feedback form](feedback-form.md).

Close with the three places to look: the dashboard guide, the glossary, the registry.

## Adapting the workshop

- **Half-day version:** add a fourth exercise where groups build one page of a report from the dictionary, then criticise each other's caveats.
- **For a technical audience:** replace exercise 1 with a lineage trace, and use the [dictionary](../generated/data-dictionary.md) instead of the glossary.
- **For senior staff:** keep only exercises 2 and 3, and add the [decision rights](../governance/framework.md#decision-rights) table.
- **Online:** put everyone in pairs in breakout rooms for the exercises; the debrief questions work unchanged in chat if you ask "what did you see that you did not expect".
